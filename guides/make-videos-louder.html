How to make a video louder than 100% in Chrome or Edge

Some videos are mixed so quietly that full volume still isn't enough, especially on laptop speakers. Your browser can't go past 100% on its own, but there are a few ways to get more out of it.

First, check the obvious volume controls

There are usually three separate volume levels between a video and your ears, and any of them can be the one holding things back:

  1. The player on the page. YouTube and most video sites have their own volume slider, separate from your computer's. Make sure it's all the way up.
  2. The browser itself. On Windows, right-click the speaker icon in the taskbar, open the Volume mixer, and check that Chrome or Edge isn't turned down there. Browsers sometimes get set lower than other apps and stay that way.
  3. Your computer's main volume. On a Mac, also check System Settings → Sound, and that the right output (speakers or headphones) is selected.

If all three are at the top and the video is still too quiet, the audio itself is quiet. That's when you need a boost.

Why browsers stop at 100%

The volume control in a web page can only turn sound down, never above the level it was recorded at. To go louder, something has to amplify the audio before it reaches your speakers. Browsers have a built-in audio engine that can do this, but there's no setting for it, so it takes an extension.

When a boost won't work

  • Browser and store pages (like settings pages or the extension stores) can't be changed by any extension.
  • Some sites stream audio from a separate server in a way browsers don't let extensions touch. The popup tells you when this happens; the site's own volume control still works normally.
  • Very quiet dialogue under loud music is a mixing problem more than a volume one. Turning everything up makes the music louder too. Voice-focused equalizer presets help with this (they're part of Volume Booster Pro).

Quick answers

Is boosting volume bad for my speakers?

Normal use is fine. Laptop speakers have physical limits, so if you hear rattling or distortion, turn the boost down a step. With headphones, keep the overall level comfortable; louder than normal for long periods isn't good for your hearing.

Does it slow my browser down?

No. The sound is processed by the browser's own audio engine on the tab you're boosting, and nothing runs on sites you haven't set.

Does it record or send my audio anywhere?

No. Everything happens on your computer. See our privacy policy.
